# Output template

The shape below is the contract. It is ordered by **what a reader can still act on**, not
by what was most interesting to produce — which is why deadlines come before analysis and
the triage table comes before any item write-up.

**The document is forensic and advocatory, and it does not balance.** Every section from
"What the papers do not settle" onwards exists to test the officer's case or to arm one side
of the argument. The reader already has the facts; do not spend the template's space giving
them back.

Replace everything in `[square brackets]`. Keep the headings.

> - Output of any AI skill will vary depending on the underlying model used, the model version,
>   and the model effort. **Two runs may produce different output**, and any output is governed
>   under the model provider's terms and conditions.

[**This wording is fixed. Reproduce it exactly — do not paraphrase, summarise, reorder or add
to it.** The last paragraph is the reason it is fixed: output varies between runs, so the one
part of the document that must not vary is the part that states its limits.

Keep it at the very top, above the deadlines, and set it to be unmissable — bold, boxed, full
width. It is load-bearing: the two cases read with more authority than they have earned, and
they are the part most likely to be lifted, forwarded or read aloud.

**Nothing else goes in the block.** Everything further, including that findings are about
documents rather than about people, goes in the fuller terms at the foot.

**Say nothing about predetermination or the code of conduct**, at the top or at the foot.
Members know the rules they are bound by; reciting them reads as patronising and as advice
this skill is not giving.]

### Item [n] — [reference], [site]

**Scrutiny: [high/medium].** [One clause on why — this is the triage reason, and the reader
decides here whether to read on.] **Time to read properly: [estimate].**

[Six sections per item, in this order and no other: **summary → defects → claims the
recommendation rests on → community comments and their policies → the case to approve → the
case to refuse.** The two cases come last because they are built from everything above them,
and a reader can trace every ground back up the page.]

#### Summary

[**Two or three lines at most** on what is proposed. The reader has the report and the fact
pack; this is a referent for what follows, not a précis.]

[Then the recommendation and what it turns on, in one paragraph. Quote the officer's wording
where the wording matters — particularly any hedge: "on balance", "finely balanced",
"marginal".]

[**Addendum:** what it says and what it changes — recommendation, conditions, a late
consultee response, an answer to an objection. It goes here, not further down, because it
changes what every section below is about. Set it out even where it changes little. Write
"None published as at [time]" if so.]

#### Defects — what the papers do not settle

[The forensic findings. Each with a verbatim quote and a
page or paragraph reference, and each marked as one of:]
- **Wrong** — [a statement that is incorrect, with what is correct.]
- **Thin** — [a conclusion the evidence in the report does not support.]
- **Missing** — [a material consideration, document, consultation or statutory duty not
  addressed at all.]
- **Conflicting** — [the summary and the assessment, or the précis of a consultee and their
  actual response, saying different things.]
- **Unexamined alternative** — [an option on the table the report does not engage with. Name
  it and record the silence; do not appraise it.]

[Where there are several, say the report warrants a full assertion-by-assertion audit —
a different exercise from a pack review — rather than approximating one here.]

[Where there are none: say so plainly. "The report is sound and its conclusions
well-evidenced" is a finding, and a review that never says it will not be believed when it
says the opposite. Then go straight to the weighting pivots — an absence of defects is not
an absence of routes.]

**Where the weight is the member's to set.** [Same block, because it is the same act of
scrutiny: a defect is something the report got wrong; a pivot is something the report
presents as settled that is in fact a judgement.]

| Consideration | Officer's weight (quoted) | Fixed or at large | Conclusion turns on it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| [what] | ["significant weight"] | [at large / fixed by — cite what fixes it] | [yes / no] |

[Beneath the table, one line per pivot marked `yes`: what on the file would support weighting
it differently, or "nothing on the file supports a different weighting" where that is the
case.

⚠️ Also state here, where it arises, anything raised that is **not a material planning
consideration** — private views, property values, competition, the applicant's identity.
A member cannot act on it and the reader needs to know.]

#### Claims the recommendation rests on

[The applicant's evidence the report relies on. **Three or four load-bearing claims on a major
application; often none on a householder one.** A claim earns a place only if the
recommendation moves when the claim moves — an inventory of submitted documents buries the
ones that matter.

⚠️ **Do not re-run the technical work.** Not "the noise assessment is wrong", not a recomputed
metric, not a disputed traffic count. Say what the claim is, what depends on it, who tested
it, and what question would test it.]

| The claim | Whose | What depends on it | Who tested it |
|---|---|---|---|
| [quoted or closely paraphrased, with its document] | [applicant's consultant, by discipline — never by name] | [the conclusion, with its paragraph] | [**verified** / **accepted without examination** / **hedged** — quote the hedge / **untested**] |

[Beneath the table, one line per claim: **the question that would test it** — a single
question, answerable at the meeting or by a condition.

Watch for these four shapes and name them where they appear:
- a **modelled future** presented as a finding (predicted noise, trips, water use) — the
  assumptions decide the answer;
- a **benefit in the balance that nothing secures** — read the benefit, then look for it in
  the conditions and heads of terms, and **say plainly when it is not there**;
- a **gain deferred to a post-permission condition** — the committee is weighing a promise;
- an **impossibility asserted rather than demonstrated** ("on-site mitigation is not
  possible") — usually the applicant's position in the report's voice.

Where the report relies on a document that is not in the pack, say so here and label it as
outside what the committee has.]

#### Community comments, and the policy each attaches to

[Ranked strongest first. Each numbered entry carries: the concern in one line, the **policy
it anchors to — by number for an adopted plan policy, by code for a national one**, whether
anything on the file supports it, and whether the report engages with it or only records it.

This section exists so that a member who thinks the community has a point can cite the
policy out loud in the chamber. An entry with no policy number has failed its job.]

1. **[Concern]** — [Policy [number/code]]. [Evidenced by / asserted by.] [Report engages /
   records only / silent.]

**Raised, but without a planning anchor.** [Factually and respectfully: not a material
consideration / material but already secured by condition [n] / contradicted by [the
consultee with the remit]. Do not omit these — an objector who is told which points will not
land is better served than one who is not.]

[**Parish or town council position**, stated separately — a statutory consultee, never folded
into a volume count.]

[Where the pack contains only the officer's summary of the representations rather than the
representations themselves, **say so here**: the rankings inherit whatever the summary left
out.]

[The two cases below are written **independently of each other**. Each is the strongest
available case for that outcome, as an advocate for it would put it, drawing on everything
above. Neither concedes to the other, both get the same effort, and there is no closing
sentence weighing them up.]

#### If you are minded to approve, you can do so on the basis…
[Numbered. Each ground carries: the policy it hangs on, and whether it is evidenced on the
file or asserted — and by whom. Where the ground depends on a particular weighting, say so:
"this requires giving the housing contribution the weight the report gives it, which is at
large". State them; do not weigh them.]

#### If you are minded to refuse, the strongest reasons are…

[Numbered, same requirements, plus: whether a condition or obligation could cure it — stated
as a fact, with no costs or appeal prediction attached.

A community concern anchored to a policy above belongs here, carried over with its policy
and identified as a point the community raised.

⚠️ **Never forecast what an appeal would do.** No "would not survive an appeal", no "an
inspector would be likely to", no risk rating. Describe the ground; do not predict its fate.
Members determine their own approach.

Where the papers do not support a ground, say so as a fact about the papers — "no ground on
these papers carries both a policy anchor and evidence on the file" — and then say what each
candidate actually rests on: foreclosed by an allocation, no objection from the consultee
with the remit, curable by condition, not a material consideration. List those separately
from the main list, so a member can see what was considered.

**Before writing that, check the pivots table.** A route via re-weighting is still a route,
and where one exists it goes in this list stated as what it is: "the route here is not a
defect in the report but the weight given to [x], which the report treats as a judgement and
on which its conclusion turns."

Do not close with a sentence weighing the two lists against each other. The reader weighs
them.]

## Notes on using this template

**Length discipline.** The whole point is that it is read the night before a meeting. Two
pages of triage plus a section per flagged item beats fifteen pages of thoroughness that
nobody finishes.

**Bullets, not semicolon-runs** (house rule 9). Enumerations of three or more go in a list,
never strung through a paragraph. One point per paragraph, paragraphs short.

**Quote, do not characterise.** Every criticism carries the officer's own words. A reader
who disagrees with the judgement should be able to see exactly what was said without
opening the pack.

**Do not balance.** Nowhere in this template is there a section that weighs the case for
against the case against, and none should be added. If the output is drifting toward "on
balance", it is producing the officer report a second time — hand the reader to
`planning-balance` or `policy-compliance-assessment` instead.

**Every policy gets a number.** "The local plan supports this" is not usable at a committee
meeting. `DP12` is. This applies hardest in the community-representations section, which
exists precisely to convert a concern into something a member can cite.

**Names.** Report the substance of objections, not the identities of objectors, and never
reproduce addresses or contact details from the pack. Officers and members act in a public
capacity and may be named where the point is about a decision they made.
