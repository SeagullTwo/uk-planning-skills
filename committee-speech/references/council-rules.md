# Council rules — what to find, and where

Reference file for `committee-speech`. **Every figure in this file is a blank to be filled from
the council's own constitution.** None of it can be defaulted, and none of it can be carried
over from another council.

## Why this file has no numbers in it

Speaking rules vary by council, by committee tier within a council, and over time as
constitutions are revised. A worked example from a real council would be copied as fact, and the
first thing to go out of date would be the registration deadline — which is the one figure where
being wrong costs the user their slot entirely.

So: **the shape is here; the instances are not.** Find them, and say in the deliverable that you
did.

## What to establish

### 1. Which committee

Councils commonly run **more than one planning committee**, split by a threshold — scheme size,
dwelling count, floorspace, or a geographic area. A large scheme goes to a different committee
from a householder application, **with different speaking rules**.

Find: the committees that exist, and the threshold that decides which one hears this item.

Getting this wrong means preparing against the wrong slot count and the wrong clock.

### 2. The speaking budget

- **How many objector slots?** Commonly one to three.
- **How many minutes each?** Commonly two to four.
- **Is there a separate slot for a parish or town council representative?** Usually yes, and it
  is additional to the objector slots rather than part of them.
- **Do supporters get equal slots?** Usually. Relevant because it tells the speaker what will be
  said after them.
- **Is the time per speaker, or shared across the objectors?** This changes the whole allocation
  — a shared pool means the slate has one budget, not three.

### 3. Registration — the deadline, and the mechanism

⚠️ **This is the one that ends the exercise if it is wrong.**

- **The deadline to register to speak.** Typically several working days before the meeting, and
  often expressed as a time on a named working day rather than a date.
- **How to register** — a form, an email address, a phone number, a portal.
- **Whether the text must be submitted in advance**, and by when. Some councils require the
  words ahead of the meeting; that changes the deliverable and it changes the deadline that
  matters.
- **Who may speak** — whether the slot is restricted to those who made a written representation
  during consultation, which is common and catches people out.

**Never supply a plausible-looking deadline.** A wrong deadline is acted on; an absent one is
checked. If it has not been established, say so in the deliverable and send the user to the
council's page.

### 4. The rule on departing from the officer's recommendation

Most constitutions impose requirements where members decide against the recommendation, and the
shape is usually some combination of:

- proper regard to the officer's professional advice;
- reasons that are recorded;
- sometimes an explanation of alternatives considered;
- occasionally a **deferral or cooling-off requirement** before the decision takes effect.

Find the article and what it actually says. **Do not assert a cooling-off period unless the
constitution contains one** — many do not, and a speaker who invents one will be corrected.

**And deliver it as a light aside, never a warning** — see principle 6 in `SKILL.md`. The
difference between *"as you know, you'd need to record your reasons"* and a recitation of the
duty with the appeal costs attached is the difference between help and a lecture.

### 5. Procedural points worth knowing

- **The order of speaking** — objectors before or after the applicant. Speaking first means not
  being able to answer; speaking last means being able to.
- **Whether members may ask the speaker questions**, and whether time spent answering comes out
  of the slot.
- **Whether the item may be deferred at the meeting**, and on whose motion.
- **Whether the meeting is webcast** — worth telling the speaker, who may not expect it.

## Where to find it

1. **The council's committee system**, which is a democratic-services platform on a different
   hostname from the planning portal. `committee-pack-review` and its
   `references/finding-the-pack.md` cover how to reach it. The agenda frontsheet and any
   "procedure for public speaking" document published with the pack are the most reliable
   source, because they are current as at that meeting.
2. **The constitution itself**, usually published on the council's website under a "constitution"
   or "how the council works" heading. Look for the planning committee's procedure rules and the
   article on decision-making principles.
3. **The council's "speaking at planning committee" guidance page**, which is written for the
   public and is usually the clearest — but check it against the constitution where the two
   could differ.

## Record what you found

In the deliverable, state the committee, the slot count, the time limit and the registration
deadline **with the date you established them**, so the speaker knows how fresh the figures are
and a later reader can tell whether they need rechecking.

Where any of them could not be established, **say which**, rather than filling the gap with a
plausible value.
