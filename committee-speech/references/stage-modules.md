# Stage modules

Reference file for `committee-speech`. **Read the module that matches before writing a word**,
and lift its test into the opening frame.

The stage decides what the committee may lawfully consider. Arguing a point the stage has
foreclosed is the commonest and most expensive mistake a speaker makes: the officer corrects it
in the reply, in front of the members, and the rest of the speech is heard through that.

⚠️ **Policy codes and weight directions change between framework editions.** Everything below is
the *shape* of the argument. Verify the current wording and numbering through
`national-planning-policy` before any of it is spoken.

---

## Reserved matters

**What is settled.** The principle of development, the quantum, the land uses and anything else
fixed at outline are settled and **cannot be reopened**. Concede this at the top. Arguing it
wastes the one thing an objector cannot replace, which is the room's willingness to believe the
next thing they say.

**What the committee is deciding.** Only the matters reserved — typically appearance, layout,
scale and landscaping. That is a narrower fight, and the narrowness is the opportunity: on those
matters the committee has real discretion and the officer's view is one view.

**What compels refusal.** Only two things, and usually neither is absolute:

1. **A reserved matter that departs from the outline permission or its approved parameter
   plans.** It cannot lawfully be approved as submitted. If this is live, lead with it — it is
   the strongest position an objector at reserved matters can hold.
2. **A statutory duty that cannot be overcome** on the matters reserved.

**Where the argument actually is.** The weight of design, heritage, landscape and amenity harm
on the reserved matters. Anchor to the council's own advisers wherever they have commented.

**Conditions.** Any offered must relate to the reserved matters and must not conflict with the
outline permission. A condition that contradicts the outline is not available and offering one
signals the speaker has not read the permission.

## Outline

**The principle is live.** This is the widest fight and the one where the framework does most
work.

**Fix the location first.** Whether the site is inside or outside a defined settlement governs
which national policy applies and therefore what the applicant has to show. Establish it before
anything else, because the whole argument hangs off it.

**Outside a settlement**, housing typically has to qualify through a specific gateway — an
evidenced unmet need, such as an absent five-year housing land supply. **The provisos attached
to that gateway are usually the strongest objector ground available**, because they are
conditions the applicant must satisfy rather than harms the objector must prove. Look for
requirements that the site be physically well related to an existing settlement, and of a scale
the infrastructure can accommodate. Fail a proviso and the applicant is thrown back on a higher
test.

**What compels refusal.** A national decision-making policy that directs refusal in the
circumstances — protected landscapes, irreplaceable habitat or SSSI, substantial harm to a
designated heritage asset, the flood-risk sequential and exception tests — or a proposal that
fits no qualifying category at all, or a statutory bar such as the Habitats Regulations.

**Where none of those bites**, it is a balance: adverse effects against benefits. **Confront the
housing-supply position directly rather than hoping it will not come up.** A shortfall engages a
pro-development balance, the officer's report will say so, and a speech that ignores it sounds
like it has not read the report.

**Emerging plans cut both ways.** If the applicant leans on a draft allocation, the adopted plan
carries full statutory weight and the emerging one carries only what its stage, its unresolved
objections and its consistency with the framework allow. Prematurity is a refusal ground only in
narrow circumstances. Often decisive at outline — and worth checking whether it helps or hurts
before raising it.

## Full and householder

**The whole merits are open.** The test is s.38(6) — decide in accordance with the development
plan unless material considerations indicate otherwise — plus the framework.

Treat it as the outline module without the settlement-gateway analysis: name the development
plan policies breached, by number, weigh the harms, and offer conditions.

**On householder applications, keep the scale of the speech proportionate to the scale of the
decision.** A three-minute speech against a rear extension that opens with statutory
construction will lose the room. The neighbour's actual experience of the harm, tied to one
amenity policy, is worth more than a policy tour.

---

## Appeal items

**The committee is not the decision-maker.** The Planning Inspectorate is. An item before
members at appeal stage is usually about the council's case: whether to defend, what reasons to
advance, or a statement to be submitted.

The speech should therefore go to **the reasons, the development-plan conflict, the weight of
harm, and the cost risk** — and should stay inside the main issues as the Inspector has framed
them. Points outside those issues will not be determined and spending a slot on them wastes it.

This is the one stage where the audience is being asked to decide what *the council* argues
rather than what happens to the application, and the speech should be pitched accordingly.
