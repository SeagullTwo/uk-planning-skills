# Flood-risk & drainage policy and guidance (England)

The framework to cite when mapping a deficiency (skill function 2). **Cite the specific
instrument and clause, never "best practice" in the abstract.** Each deficiency in
[`deficiency-catalogue.md`](deficiency-catalogue.md) points here.

The **current NPPF/PPG edition register, the verify-before-citing protocol, and the shared
decision-making core** (s.38(6) and plan primacy, the presumption in favour of sustainable
development, emerging-plan weight, the conditions and obligations tests) live in the companion
**national-planning-policy** skill — check it first when citing the NPPF/PPG. This file owns
only the topic-specific layer.

## Part 1 — NPPF (Chapter 18, "Managing flood risk and coastal change")

**17 August 2026 edition** — the Framework was republished as **coded policies**: chapter 18
carries the flood and coastal-change policies (**F1–F3** plan-making; **F4–F9** national
decision-making policies), and **Annex F ("Managing Flood Risk and Coastal Change")** carries
the flood zones, the vulnerability classification and the zone-compatibility table — all now
*inside* the Framework. Verified **18 August 2026** against the official PDF (⏳ at that date
gov.uk offered the PDF only; the HTML version was pending). The edition register, the
verify-before-citing protocol and the crosswalk from the December 2024 paragraphs (170–182)
live in the companion **national-planning-policy** skill — including the rule that
**applications determined before 17 August 2026** are read against the edition in force at
determination (do not re-cite the new codes retrospectively).

- **Chapter 18 objective — avoid risk / safe for lifetime:** minimise risks "from all sources of
  flooding and coastal change, taking into account the impacts of climate change, by steering
  development away from areas of risk, ensuring that development will be **safe for its lifetime
  without increasing flood risk elsewhere**, and incorporating sustainable drainage systems
  where appropriate."
- **F4 — when a site-specific FRA is required.** *The trigger is now policy, not a footnote*
  (formerly Dec 2024 footnote 63). **F4(1):** an FRA "should accompany **all** development
  proposals in **Flood Zones 2, 3a, and 3b**" (as defined in Annex F, table 1). **F4(2):** in
  **Zone 1**, an FRA is required for **(a)** sites of **one hectare or more**; **(b)** land the
  Environment Agency has identified as having **critical drainage problems**; and **(c)** land
  identified in a **strategic flood risk assessment or the Flood Map for Planning** as at risk
  of flooding **from any source, now or in the future**. **F4(3):** proportionate to the scale,
  nature and location of the development, and supporting the sequential/exception-test
  assessment where relevant.
- **F5 — the Sequential Test:** the aim is "to steer new development to areas with the lowest
  risk of flooding from any source"; proposals "should not be located in areas at risk of
  flooding where **alternative sites, appropriate for the development, are reasonably available
  in areas with a lower risk of flooding**" (F5(1)). (The first hurdle — can be decisive on its
  own.) **New limit:** the area of search "should not be greater than the **anticipated
  catchment** of the development in terms of its likely occupiers or users." **F5(2)** — the
  test applies in areas known to be at risk **now or in the future from any form of flooding**
  (surface water in scope), *except*: **(a)** sites **allocated in the development plan** that
  were sequentially tested at plan stage; **(b)** where an FRA demonstrates clearly that
  **(i)** no built development (including access/escape routes, land-raising or other vulnerable
  elements) would be on land at risk from any source, now or in future, or **(ii)** for
  **surface-water-only** risk, that layout, design and mitigation keep occupiers safe for the
  development's lifetime without increasing risk elsewhere (per F7); and **(c)** householder
  development, small non-residential extensions (footprint **<250 m²**) and changes of use
  (other than to a caravan, camping or chalet site, or a mobile-/park-home site). **F5(3):**
  the SFRA and the Flood Map for Planning are the evidential basis for the test.
- **F6(1)(a) — incompatible uses are refused outright:** in Zones 2, 3a or 3b, "development
  proposals **should be refused** if the use is incompatible with the risk from river or sea
  flooding, as set out in Annex F, table 3" — a refusal directive that bites before any
  exception test (e.g. *highly vulnerable* in Zone 3a/3b; *more* or *less vulnerable* in 3b).
- **F6(1)(b)–(2) — the Exception Test (now THREE criteria, all required):** where Annex F
  table 3 requires it — in addition to the Sequential Test where applicable — development is
  only appropriate where it would **(i)** "provide **wider sustainability benefits to the
  community that outweigh the flood risk**"; **(ii)** "be **safe for its anticipated lifetime**
  in accordance with policy F7"; and **(iii)** "**not increase flood risk elsewhere** and, where
  possible, **reduce flood risk overall**." *Do not present this as the old two-part test* —
  safety-for-lifetime and not-increasing-risk-elsewhere are now **separate criteria, each
  failable on its own**. **F6(2):** the test must be informed by a site-specific FRA; it is not
  needed where the Sequential Test is not required (per F5), nor on a plan-allocated site
  already exception-tested — unless flood risk has since increased significantly or the scheme
  is more vulnerable than what was allocated (Annex F, table 2).
- **F7 — safe development (refusal-framed):** F7(1): proposals "should not present a risk from
  flooding to potential occupiers, users, or visitors, and should not increase flood risk
  elsewhere." F7(2): where development is proposed in a location at risk from any form of
  flooding, now or in future, "it **should be refused unless**": **(a)** within the site, the
  **most vulnerable development is in the areas of lowest flood risk** (within-site sequential
  approach — absent overriding reasons); **(b)** it will be **safe throughout its lifetime**
  taking account of the **vulnerability of its users**; **(c)** any **residual risk** can be
  safely managed, with **safe access and escape** routes as part of an **agreed emergency
  plan**; **(d)** it is appropriately **flood resistant and resilient** (after a flood, quickly
  back into use without significant refurbishment); and **(e)** it is demonstrated that flood
  risk will **not be increased elsewhere**. (SuDS is no longer one of these strands — it is a
  separate policy, F8.)
- **F8 — SuDS and watercourses:** proposals which could affect drainage on or around the site
  "should incorporate **sustainable drainage systems** to control flow rates and reduce volumes
  of runoff", proportionately, with multifunctional benefits (water quality, biodiversity,
  amenity) where possible (F8(1)). SuDS should **(a)** "be **designed in accordance with the
  National Standards for Sustainable Drainage Systems**" (F8(2)(a) — the 2025 Standards, Part 4,
  are now hooked directly into the Framework); **(b)** have **maintenance arrangements** for the
  development's anticipated lifetime; and **(c)** for **major development**, take account of
  **LLFA advice**. **F8(3) (new):** proposals should not enclose existing watercourses without
  compelling reasons, and should where possible **remove existing culverts and renaturalise
  river channels** (unless that would increase flood risk or cause environmental or heritage
  harm) — cite this against culverting-led drainage layouts.
- **Annex F, table 1 — the flood zones, now defined in the Framework:** **Zone 1** (low, <0.1%
  annual probability); **Zone 2** (medium, 0.1–1% river / 0.1–0.5% sea); **Zone 3 is now split
  in the Framework itself** — **Zone 3a** (high, ≥1% river / ≥0.5% sea) and **Zone 3b**
  (functional floodplain: land that has to flow or store flood water, normally ≥3.3% annual
  probability or land *designed* to flood, identified in the SFRA in agreement with the EA —
  **not shown separately from 3a on the Flood Map for Planning**). The table's note repeats that
  the mapped zones **ignore defences and climate change** — consult the EA's other data and the
  SFRA.
- **Annex F, table 2 — Flood risk vulnerability classification:** the five categories —
  *essential infrastructure, highly vulnerable, more vulnerable, less vulnerable,
  water-compatible* — with updated membership (essential infrastructure now includes e.g. data
  centres, EV charging stations, solar farms, wind turbines, heat networks). Mixed-use schemes
  take the **highest** applicable class unless assessed in component parts (table 3 notes).
- **Annex F, table 3 — vulnerability × zone "incompatibility" matrix** (formerly PPG Table 3):
  **refuse (X, via F6(1)(a)):** *highly vulnerable* in Zone 3a or 3b; *more vulnerable* and
  *less vulnerable* in Zone 3b. **Exception Test required:** *highly vulnerable* in Zone 2;
  *more vulnerable* in Zone 3a; *essential infrastructure* in Zones 3a and 3b (subject to the
  † and * design notes — in 3b, essential infrastructure and water-compatible uses must remain
  operational and safe in flood, cause **no net loss of floodplain storage**, and not impede
  flows or increase risk elsewhere).

*Do not mis-cite the NPPF:* the flood policies still do **not** itemise the six flood sources,
do not use the term "climate change allowances" (only "current and future impacts of climate
change"), and do not set out the drainage hierarchy — those remain PPG / technical-standards
material (Parts 2, 4). But two old cautions have **inverted** in this edition: the national SuDS
standards **are** now named in the Framework (F8(2)(a)), and the flood zones and
vulnerability/compatibility tables **are** now Framework text (Annex F) — cite Annex F, not the
PPG tables, as the primary source. Never cite "paragraphs 170–182" against the current edition;
those are December 2024 numbers.

## Part 2 — Planning Practice Guidance ("Flood risk and coastal change")
gov.uk/guidance/flood-risk-and-coastal-change (paragraph Ref IDs `7-…`)

> ⏳ **Not yet updated for the August 2026 NPPF** at verification (18 Aug 2026): the PPG flood
> pages still keyed to the December 2024 paragraph/footnote numbering, and their flood-zone and
> vulnerability tables are now restated as **NPPF Annex F tables 1–3** — cite the Framework as
> the primary source (it prevails where they differ) and use the PPG for procedure. Re-check
> whether the PPG has caught up with the F-codes before citing it alongside them.

- **Flood Zones (Table 1, `7-078`):** now restated as **NPPF Annex F table 1** (Part 1 above) —
  cite the Framework; the PPG table and text remain useful background on how 3b is identified
  via the SFRA. The Flood Map ignores defences and **does not account for climate change** —
  consult the SFRA too.
- **When a site-specific FRA is required (`7-020`):** defers to the NPPF trigger — which is now
  **policy F4(1)–(2)**, no longer a footnote. ⏳ The PPG's cross-reference ("footnote 55", which
  the Dec 2024 edition had renumbered to 63) is now two editions out of date — cite F4.
  Assessments must be **proportionate to the degree of flood risk (`7-021`)** (mirrored in
  F4(3)).
- **Sequential Test (`7-023`–`7-029`, `7-035`):** procedural glosses on what is now policy F5:
  **existing flood defences are initially ignored** (uncertain long-term funding, `7-024`);
  applies to **major and non-major** development; the **applicant demonstrates, the LPA
  decides**; a defined **area of search (`7-027a`)** — ⏳ read with the new F5(1) cap (no wider
  than the development's anticipated catchment); a "**reasonably available**" site is one that
  is suitable, meets the same need, and has a reasonable prospect of coming forward at the same
  time (need not be owned by the applicant, `7-028`); the **absence of a 5-year housing land
  supply is not relevant** to the Sequential Test. (The allocated-site carve-out and the
  SFRA/Flood-Map evidential basis are now in the Framework itself — F5(2)(a), F5(3).)
- **Exception Test (`7-031`, `7-035`):** ⏳ the PPG still describes the old **two-part** test —
  the Framework now sets **three criteria, all required (F6(1)(b))**; cite F6. The PPG's
  discipline survives: the Exception Test is "**not a tool to justify development when the
  Sequential Test has already shown there are reasonably available, lower-risk sites**" — the
  Sequential Test comes first (mirrored in F6(1)(b): "in addition to satisfying the sequential
  test").
- **Vulnerability × flood-zone compatibility matrix (Table 2, `7-079`):** now restated as
  **NPPF Annex F tables 2–3** (Part 1 above) — cite the Framework versions; incompatible boxes
  are an outright refusal directive under F6(1)(a). Cite this where a vulnerable use is proposed
  in the wrong zone.

## Part 3 — Environment Agency
- **Statutory consultee** (Town and Country Planning (DMPO) (England) Order 2015, SI 2015/595,
  Sch 4): the LPA must consult the EA before permitting development within 20 m of a main river,
  non-minor development in Flood Zones 2/3, vulnerable uses in flood zones, and Zone 1 sites in
  EA-notified critical drainage areas. Read the EA's response first and build on any objection.
- **Flood map for planning** (flood-map-for-planning.service.gov.uk) — the **authoritative**
  flood zones for planning (ignores defences; no climate change). Do **not** cite the public
  "Check your long term flood risk" service for planning zones.
- **Flood risk assessment: standing advice** (LPA- and applicant-facing; updated 2026) — the
  benchmark for when an FRA is required and its content; the yardstick for a deficient/missing
  FRA. gov.uk/guidance/flood-risk-assessment-standing-advice
- **Climate-change allowances** — peak river-flow / peak-rainfall / sea-level allowances by
  catchment and epoch. ⏳ The guidance *page* is unchanged since 2022, but the **allowance
  datasets were refreshed (Sep 2025)** — take specific figures from the live datasets, not the
  page. gov.uk/guidance/flood-risk-assessments-climate-change-allowances

## Part 4 — Lead Local Flood Authority (LLFA) and SuDS
- **LLFA — statutory consultee** on surface-water drainage for **major development** since **15
  April 2015** (WMS HCWS161; SI 2015/595). "Major" = 10+ dwellings, or residential on ≥0.5 ha
  where the number is unknown, or ≥1,000 m² floorspace, or a site ≥1 ha.
- **National standards for sustainable drainage systems (SuDS)** — ⚠ **Defra, June 2025 (updated
  July 2025), which REPLACED the March 2015 non-statutory technical standards** at the same URL.
  Still non-statutory, but **NPPF policy F8(2)(a) now requires SuDS to be "designed in
  accordance with" them** — a direct Framework hook a representation can cite. Cover the
  runoff-destination hierarchy, interception, peak-flow control, water quality, amenity and
  biodiversity, and maintenance.
  gov.uk/government/publications/sustainable-drainage-systems-non-statutory-technical-standards
- **CIRIA "The SuDS Manual" (C753, 2015)** — the principal UK technical benchmark (no successor
  as of 2026). Key concepts to test a drainage strategy against:
  - the **SuDS management train** — prevention → source control → site control → regional
    control (manage runoff in stages, most local first);
  - the **drainage hierarchy** — (1) to ground / infiltration / soakaway; (2) to a watercourse;
    (3) to a surface-water sewer; (4) to a combined sewer (last resort);
  - **greenfield runoff rates** — restrict post-development rate *and* volume toward the
    pre-development (greenfield) rate so downstream risk is not increased.

## Part 5 — Statutory duties, foul drainage, watercourses, reservoirs
- ⏳ **Schedule 3 to the Flood and Water Management Act 2010 (mandatory SuDS / SuDS Approving
  Body) is NOT commenced in England** as of Aug 2026 — the Government's stated preference is the
  planning-policy route. **Rely on the NPPF SuDS policy (F8) and the 2025 National Standards
  it invokes, not on any statutory SAB duty in England.** (It *is* in force in Wales.)
- **Flood and Water Management Act 2010:** s.6 defines the LLFA; s.9 (local flood-risk
  management strategy); s.19 (duty to investigate flooding); s.21 (register of flood-risk
  structures). The EA leads the national FCERM strategy (s.7).
- **Ordinary watercourse consent — Land Drainage Act 1991 s.23** (not "FWMA s.23"): no
  culvert/weir/obstruction to an ordinary watercourse without written LLFA/IDB consent — relevant
  where culverting is proposed.
- **Foul drainage:** PPG "Water supply, wastewater and water quality" — the **first presumption
  is connection to the public foul sewer** (package plants/septic tanks only where a connection
  is impractical); support early engagement with the sewerage undertaker and phasing/refusal
  where capacity works don't fit the timescale. The right to connect (Water Industry Act 1991
  s.106) is **qualified** (the undertaker may refuse where prejudicial) and does **not** guarantee
  downstream capacity — use this to rebut "there's an automatic right to connect, so capacity is
  irrelevant." **Building Regulations Approved Document H** ranks surface-water discharge
  (soakaway → watercourse → sewer) in **H3** (H1 is foul).
- **Reservoir flood risk** (Reservoirs Act 1975; EA reservoir flood maps) — treat as a
  **residual risk** relevant to emergency planning and safe access/egress, not an absolute bar.
