## Part 1 — Primary legislation (the statutory duties — invoke the right one)

**Planning (Listed Buildings and Conservation Areas) Act 1990.** These are **duties**, not
policy — and the courts read them as requiring more than neutral weighing.

- **s.66(1) — listed buildings (the key duty for a *planning* application):** in considering
  whether to grant permission for development which **affects a listed building or its
  setting**, the authority **"shall have special regard to the desirability of preserving the
  building or its setting or any features of special architectural or historic interest which
  it possesses."** Per *Barnwell Manor* (Part 5), "special regard" requires **considerable
  importance and weight** and a **strong (rebuttable) presumption against** harm.
- **s.16(2) — listed building consent:** the same "special regard" duty applies to an **LBC**
  application (works to a listed building).
- **s.72(1) — conservation areas:** for any planning decision on land in a conservation area,
  **"special attention shall be paid to the desirability of preserving or enhancing the
  character or appearance of that area."** "Preserving" means **doing no harm** (*South
  Lakeland*, Part 5) — a neutral scheme satisfies the duty; a harmful one does not.
- **s.69 — designation:** LPAs designate (and must review) conservation areas as "areas of
  special architectural or historic interest the character or appearance of which it is
  desirable to preserve or enhance."
- *Invoke the right duty for the consent type:* s.16 for LBC; s.66 for planning permission
  affecting a listed building/setting; s.72 for conservation areas.
- *Source:* legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1990/9 (ss.16, 66, 69, 72).

**Ancient Monuments and Archaeological Areas Act 1979.** Scheduled monuments (s.1 schedule)
have their **own consent regime** — **scheduled monument consent (s.2)** is required for most
works and planning permission does **not** authorise them. Scheduled monuments are "assets of
the highest significance" in NPPF terms. legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/46

**Registered parks & gardens, registered battlefields, World Heritage Sites.** Their weight
flows from **NPPF policy, not a bespoke consent regime** (registered parks/gardens are
statutory under the 1953 Act s.8C but add no consent control; battlefields and WHS are
policy). All four are **"designated heritage assets"** in the NPPF Glossary (Annex B), so the
NPPF harm tests (HE6, with HE8 for World Heritage Sites) apply.

## Part 2 — NPPF (Chapter 20, "Conserving and enhancing the historic environment": policies HE1–HE10)

**17 August 2026 edition.** The Framework is now **coded policies in themed chapters**, not
sequential paragraphs — **every pre-August-2026 "para 19x/20x" citation is stale**. Chapter 20
splits **plan-making policies (HE1–HE3)** from **national decision-making policies
(HE4–HE10)**; cite the decision-making codes for a representation. ⏳ Verified against the
official PDF 18 Aug 2026 (gov.uk PDF-only at that date; HTML pending — re-check).
Dec 2024 → Aug 2026 map: the national-planning-policy skill's `references/nppf-crosswalk-2026.md`.

**Two changes of substance to internalise before arguing anything:** (1) the weight duty is
now **"substantial weight"**, not "great weight" — do not quote "great weight" from this
edition; and (2) the **"less than substantial harm" category label is abolished** — there is
no two-category gateway; HE6(4) weighs *any* harm against public benefits, and only the
defined **substantial harm / total loss** threshold (HE5(2)(c)–(d)) switches in the stricter
HE6(5) test. Use the old vocabulary only for decisions made under the old edition.

- **HE4(1) — conserve in a manner appropriate to significance:** proposals affecting the
  significance of heritage assets, **including any contribution made by their setting**,
  should maintain/secure a use consistent with conservation and **"avoid (or if that is not
  possible, minimise) harm"**.
- **HE4(2) [was para 213, first limb] — clear and convincing justification:** "any harm to,
  or loss of, the significance of a designated heritage asset (including from development
  within its setting) should have a clear and convincing justification".
- **HE4(3) [was para 209] — deliberate neglect/damage:** the deteriorated state of the asset
  must not be taken into account in any decision relating to it.
- **HE4(4) — enabling development** (now in the Framework, formerly PPG/GPA4 territory):
  judged on whether the conservation benefits outweigh the disbenefits of departing from the
  policies concerned.
- **HE5(1) [was para 207] — applicant describes significance (proportionately):** an
  assessment of significance (including setting) and of the proposal's effect on it, in detail
  "proportionate to the assets' importance and no more than is necessary"; the Historic
  Environment Record must be referred to, and appropriate expertise employed where necessary.
  Still the hook for "no proportionate significance/setting assessment provided."
- **HE5(2) [was para 208, restructured] — the assessment must CLASSIFY the effect:** positive
  effect / no effect / **harm** / **total loss** — and **"substantial harm" is defined for the
  first time**: harm that "would **seriously affect a key element of the asset's
  significance**" (HE5(2)(c)). An assessment that never states which class the effect falls
  into, or where on the harm scale it sits, is deficient. (Pre-2026 case-law glosses of the
  substantial-harm threshold belong to the old framework — argue from the policy definition.)
- **HE5(3) — effect, not scale:** "it is the effect on a heritage asset's significance rather
  than the scale of the development which should be considered" — answers "it's only a small
  scheme".
- **HE5(4) — decision-makers "should be satisfied that assessments accurately reflect the
  effects"** — the LPA-side duty; the hook for accepting a flawed Heritage Statement at face
  value.
- **HE5(5) [was para 217] — archaeology before determination:** where a proposal involves (or
  may involve) an asset with archaeological interest, an **appropriate desk-based assessment**
  and, where necessary, a **field evaluation**, used to understand significance and inform
  design/mitigation/implementation.
- **HE6(1) [was para 212] — SUBSTANTIAL WEIGHT:** "substantial weight should be given to the
  asset's conservation (and the more important the asset, the greater the weight should be)"
  — irrespective of whether the effect is positive, harm, substantial harm or total loss.
  **The phrase "great weight" is not in this edition — don't quote it.**
- **HE6(2) — positive effects on designated assets "should be supported."**
- **HE6(3) — any harm "a matter of considerable importance and weight":** the *Barnwell
  Manor* formulation is now written into policy, to be dealt with under HE6(4)–(6).
- **HE6(4) [was para 215] — ANY harm weighed against public benefits:** where a proposal
  would harm the significance of a designated asset, the effect "should be weighed against
  any public benefits" — no category label, no gateway; the HE6(1) and HE6(3) weight still
  applies inside the balance. **New:** the policy names example benefits — "securing the
  long-term reuse of a vacant or underused listed building, and enabling energy efficiency
  and low carbon heating measures". ("Optimum viable use" no longer appears in the Framework
  — cite the PPG for it, Part 3.)
- **HE6(5) [was para 214] — the substantial harm / total loss test:** refuse unless the harm
  is **necessary to achieve substantial public benefits that outweigh the harm or loss**, OR
  **all four** of: (a) the asset's nature prevents all reasonable uses of the site; (b) no
  suitable use of the asset itself can be found in the medium term through appropriate
  marketing; (c) conservation by grant-funding or not-for-profit/charitable/public ownership
  is not possible; and (d) the harm/loss is outweighed by bringing the asset back into use.
- **HE6(6) [was para 213, second limb] — exceptional / wholly exceptional:** substantial
  harm/total loss to **grade II listed buildings or grade II registered parks/gardens** should
  be **exceptional**; to **assets of the highest significance** (scheduled monuments,
  protected wreck sites, registered battlefields, grade I and II* listed buildings, grade I
  and II* registered parks & gardens, World Heritage Sites) **wholly exceptional**. Footnote
  64: non-designated assets of archaeological interest of scheduled-monument-equivalent
  significance are treated under the designated-asset policies.
- **HE7 [was para 216] — non-designated heritage assets:** positive effects "should be
  supported" (HE7(1)); harm weighed via a **"balanced judgement … having regard to the scale
  of any harm or loss and the significance of the non-designated heritage asset"** (HE7(2));
  **new** — substantial harm/total loss of a non-designated asset should only be supported
  where outweighed by the benefits (HE7(3)).
- **HE8 — World Heritage Sites:** design must pay particular regard to Outstanding Universal
  Value including setting and any buffer zone (HE8(1)(a)); retain positive contributors where
  possible; have regard to the management plan; an impact assessment (including cumulative)
  required (HE8(1)(d)); loss of a contributing element goes through HE5/HE6 (HE8(2)).
- **HE9 — conservation areas (decisions):** retain and conserve buildings/features making a
  **positive contribution** to character or appearance where possible (HE9(1)(a)); design must
  consider the area's special interest (HE9(1)(b)); **loss of a contributing element** is
  assessed for its relative significance and contribution to the area as a whole, then via
  HE5/HE6 (HE9(2)); conserving/better-revealing proposals supported (HE9(3)). The statutory
  "preserve or enhance" duty remains s.72 (Part 1) — HE9 is the policy layer, not a
  replacement for it.
- **HE10 [was para 218] — loss of assets, recording, statues:** no approval of a loss without
  reasonable steps (conditions/obligations) securing that the replacement development proceeds
  (HE10(1)(a)); the applicant must record and advance understanding proportionately, deposited
  in the HER / a public archive — but "the ability to record evidence of our past should not
  be a **decisive** factor" in approving the loss (HE10(1)(b) — ⚠ **softened** from the old
  "should not be a factor"; quote the new wording). Archaeological assets **preserved in situ
  where feasible** (HE10(2)). Historic statues/plaques/memorials/monuments retained in situ
  where possible (HE10(3)).
- **Plan-making layer (HE1–HE3)** — cite where the *council's* evidence base is the problem:
  positive strategy and a **local heritage list** (HE1); conservation-area designations to be
  accompanied by an **adopted appraisal and management plan**, reviewed periodically (HE2(2));
  an up-to-date, publicly accessible **Historic Environment Record** (HE3).
- **Setting** is woven through HE4(1) and HE5(1)–(2) (not a single policy); the operative
  method is Historic England GPA3 (Part 4). Annex B Glossary: *setting* = "the surroundings in
  which a heritage asset is experienced. Its extent is not fixed and may change as the asset
  and its surroundings evolve" — elements may contribute positively or negatively, or be
  neutral.
- **Interaction with the S4/S5 presumption:** HE6(5) is one of the "national decision-making
  policies which state that development proposals should be refused in specific circumstances"
  that override the pro-approval balance (S4(2)(c)/S5(2)) — see the decision-making core in
  the national-planning-policy skill.

## Part 3 — Planning Practice Guidance ("Historic environment")
gov.uk/guidance/conserving-and-enhancing-the-historic-environment (Ref ID 18a; substantive
detail at 18a-015 to 18a-020)
- **Proportionate significance assessment** — decisions on a proportionate assessment of the
  particular significance of any asset affected, including its setting.
- **Setting** — how setting contributes to significance and how to assess effects (with GPA3).
- **Optimum viable use** — securing the use that keeps the asset viable while causing least
  harm to significance; a public benefit to weigh in the HE6(4) balance. ⏳ The phrase no
  longer appears in the NPPF itself (the Aug 2026 edition instead names "securing the
  long-term reuse of a vacant or underused listed building") — cite the PPG for the concept,
  and check whether the PPG page has been revised against the new edition.
- **Enabling development** — otherwise-unacceptable development justified because it secures an
  asset's conservation; now anchored in the Framework at HE4(4), with the tests in the PPG and
  Historic England GPA4.
- **Recording / the Historic Environment Record (HER)** — recording as mitigation, proportionate
  to importance, publicly deposited.

## Part 4 — Historic England guidance (current editions)
- **GPA1 — The Historic Environment in Local Plans** (2015).
- **GPA2 — Managing Significance in Decision-Taking in the Historic Environment** (2015) — the
  core decision-taking note: assess significance, understand impact, minimise harm, use HERs.
- **GPA3 — The Setting of Heritage Assets** — **2nd ed., Dec 2017** (⚠ supersedes the 2015 1st
  ed.). The **staged setting method:** Step 1 identify the assets affected; Step 2 assess
  whether/how setting contributes to significance; Step 3 assess the effect of the proposal;
  Step 4 maximise enhancement/minimise harm; Step 5 make and document the decision. Cite this
  when a setting assessment is absent or not to method.
- **GPA4 — Enabling Development and Heritage Assets** (2020).
- **HEAN 12 — Statements of Heritage Significance: Analysing Significance in Heritage Assets**
  (2019) — the benchmark for preparing/critiquing a Statement of Significance.
- **HEAN 1 (2nd ed., 2019) — Conservation Area Appraisal, Designation and Management** (⚠ this
  is HEAN 1, not "HEAN 3"). Cite alongside the local conservation-area appraisal.
- ⏳ **HEAN 4 Tall Buildings (2015)** and **HEAN 10 Listed Buildings and Curtilage (2018)** —
  relevant to townscape/long views and to curtilage; confirm current editions before citing.
  **HEAN 2 Making Changes to Heritage Assets** has a Dec 2025 revision — check the edition.

**Significance vocabulary — use the statutory one.** Lead with the NPPF's four **interests** —
**archaeological, architectural, artistic, historic** (the language of statute, the NPPF and
HEAN 12). Historic England's *Conservation Principles* (2008) four **values** — **evidential,
historical, aesthetic, communal** — are a useful analytical overlay but are not the
statutory/policy vocabulary; don't conflate the two.

## Part 5 — Case law on the statutory duties

The duties (ss.66/72) are statute and unchanged by the Aug 2026 NPPF; the cases stand. But
several were decided **against the old paragraph-numbered, two-category framework** — where a
case's NPPF hook has moved, the note says so. Don't transplant the old vocabulary ("great
weight", "less than substantial") into an argument under the current edition.

- **Barnwell Manor / East Northamptonshire DC v SSCLG [2014] EWCA Civ 137:** the **s.66(1)
  duty** requires the desirability of preserving a listed building/its setting to be given
  **"considerable importance and weight"** — a **strong presumption against** harm, not neutral
  weighing. *Framework hook:* the Aug 2026 NPPF now writes this formulation into policy —
  HE6(3): "any harm to a designated heritage asset will be a matter of considerable importance
  and weight" — so the statutory and policy weight can be argued together.
- **Jones v Mordue [2015] EWCA Civ 1243:** an LPA that **works through the NPPF's historic-
  environment policies** will "generally" have discharged the s.66(1) duty — decided on the
  old numbered paragraphs (then 133–134); the equivalent today is an officer report that
  actually applies **HE4–HE10** (and, for a pre-17-Aug-2026 decision, the paragraphs then in
  force). Insist the report applies the edition that governs.
- **Palmer v Herefordshire [2016] EWCA Civ 1061:** applies *Mordue*; an express reference to the
  NPPF heritage policies indicates compliance absent contrary evidence.
- **R (Forge Field) v Sevenoaks DC [2014] EWHC 1895 (Admin):** even modest, below-threshold
  harm attracts **considerable importance and weight**, and benefits must **clearly outweigh**
  the harm. *Framework hook:* argued in the era's "less than substantial" terms — that label
  is gone from the Aug 2026 edition, but the principle maps directly onto HE6(1)/(3)/(4): harm
  short of the substantial-harm threshold is still weighted harm inside the HE6(4) balance.
  ⏳ confirm citation before quoting.
- **South Lakeland DC v SSE [1992] (HL):** **"preserving" = doing no harm** (s.72). ⏳ confirm
  the [1992] 2 AC 141 parallel citation.
- **R (Bramshill) v SSHCLG [2021] EWCA Civ 320:** "less than substantial harm" is **not** "less
  than substantial weight"; the NPPF harm categories operate **alongside** the statutory duties,
  not in place of them. *Framework hook:* the category vocabulary it construes was abolished in
  Aug 2026, which if anything entrenches the principle — under HE6(1)/(3)–(4) the *degree* of
  harm never fixes its *weight*; substantial weight and considerable importance attach to any
  harm. Cite it for the principle, not the label.
- **Substantial-harm threshold glosses** (e.g. significance "vitiated or very much reduced")
  come from pre-2026 case law on an undefined term. The Aug 2026 Framework **defines**
  substantial harm — "would seriously affect a key element of the asset's significance"
  (HE5(2)(c)) — argue the threshold from the policy definition for current decisions; the
  judicial gloss belongs to decisions under the old editions.
