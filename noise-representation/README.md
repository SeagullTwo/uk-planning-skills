# Noise Representation

A skill for producing a rigorous, credible representation on the **noise and disturbance**
merits of a UK planning application — or advising that no sustainable objection exists.
England-focused.

> **Not legal advice. No warranty.** Output is a draft aid provided "as is". **A human must
> review and check it before submitting.** Note that a UK planning representation is normally
> **published on the council's portal in the submitter's name** and kept on the record —
> include only personal details you're content to make public.

It does three things:

1. **Evaluate** the applicant's noise evidence (Noise Impact Assessment and its appendices, BS
   4142 assessment, plant schedule, layout and boundary drawings, management plan) against
   current policy and the applicable standards, and identify the material deficiencies.
2. **Map** each deficiency to the specific national and local policy, standard or guidance it
   engages.
3. **Draft** the representation in clear, precise, concise language — to the house style of a
   strong representation.

## Three things that define this skill

- **Establish the direction first.** **Direction A** — the development is the noise *source*
  (a new or intensified noise-generating use next to existing homes). **Direction B** — the
  development is the *receptor* (new housing or other sensitive use in a noisy location). The
  policy tests differ, and the **agent-of-change** policy (NPPF **P4**) belongs to Direction B
  only: cite it in a Direction A objection and you argue against your own case.
- **Attack the method, not the decibel.** An objector has no measurements, and "it will be too
  loud" is an assertion a decision-maker cannot act on. The winnable ground is that the
  assessment does not answer the question policy **P3** asks — wrong receptor, unrepresentative
  baseline, averages with no maximum levels, character never assessed, no LOAEL/SOAEL, worst
  case never modelled, mitigation assumed rather than specified.
- **Be honest about the other regimes.** NPPF **P3(3)** and **DM7(1)** now direct the
  decision-maker to assume, *unless there is clear evidence to the contrary*, that statutory
  nuisance, permitting and licensing will operate effectively. That assumption is rebuttable —
  but only on evidence, not on the assertion that enforcement is slow. The skill says so, and
  tells you what evidence rebuts it.

## Scope

**Planning only.** Licensing (a premises licence or its variation, under the Licensing Act
2003) is a separate regime with different objectives, a short representation window and its own
procedure. Noise can matter to both, but a planning representation is not a licensing
representation — the skill signposts the boundary and does not cross it.

## Freshness

NPPF citations were quote-verified **1 September 2026** against the **17 August 2026 edition**,
whose coded policies (pollution: **P1–P6**, with noise in **P3** and agent of change in **P4**)
replaced the December 2024 paragraph numbers — every pre-August-2026 paragraph citation for
noise is stale. The PPG noise page, standards and guidance were web-verified on the same date.
Two live items flagged ⏳ in `national-guidance.md`: the **PPG noise page still carries its 22
July 2019 revision** and pre-dates the coded Framework, and **BS 8233:2014 is under revision**
(BSI project 2023-00544) with no revised edition published at verification. **Re-verify at the
point of drafting.**
