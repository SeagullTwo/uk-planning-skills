# Objection template + annotated worked example

A **skeleton** to fill, and a **worked example** showing the house style in practice. Match the
example's density and tone, not its exact wording — every point must come from the documents
actually in front of you, and every figure must be quoted from them.

## Worked example (condensed — annotations in ⟨…⟩)

⟨A **Direction A** example: an application to create an outdoor drinking and dining area at an
existing licensed premises, with an extension of hours, close to housing. Header, RE line and
opening omitted for brevity — see the skeleton. Names, places, consultants and references are
placeholders.⟩

**This representation addresses** the Noise Impact Assessment ([consultant], [date]) and its
appendices, the proposed layout drawing [ref], the plant schedule, and the Premises Management
Plan submitted in support of the application.

**The relevant framework includes:** ⟨keep to what is engaged — see national-guidance.md.
Present it as a short bulleted list, not a semicolon-run.⟩

- **NPPF policy P3** — living conditions and pollution: development should not give rise to
  unacceptable levels of noise on or beyond the site, adverse impacts being **avoided where
  possible or else mitigated to an acceptable degree** (P3(2)(b)); and should mitigate and
  **reduce to a minimum** adverse impacts from noise and **not result in noise exposure having a
  significant observed adverse effect** (P3(2)(d)), taking cumulative and off-site effects into
  account (P3(1));
- **NPPF policy DM6(1)** — conditions must be necessary, relevant, **enforceable** and
  reasonable;
- the **Planning Practice Guidance on noise** — the observed effect levels and the noise
  exposure hierarchy (*Reference ID: 30-004-20190722*), the factors influencing whether noise is
  a concern, including its **character** and distinguishability (*30-006-20190722*), and the
  mitigation types available (*30-010-20190722*);
- the **Noise Policy Statement for England** (2010) — avoid significant adverse impacts;
  mitigate and minimise adverse impacts;
- **BS 4142:2014+A1:2019** for the proposed plant, and the **IEMA Guidelines for Environmental
  Noise Impact Assessment** (v1.2, 2014), which the Assessment states it follows;
- **[the Local Plan's noise and residential amenity policies — insert the specific
  references]**, and **[the Council's technical guidance on noise and planning, if it has one]**.

### 1. The Assessment reports no maximum noise levels at any receptor, although it states that it follows a methodology which requires them

⟨The strongest and most common point. Note the shape: quote the scope, show the empty body,
bullet the omissions rather than running them together.⟩

The Assessment states at §3.2 that it has been prepared "*in accordance with the IEMA Guidelines
for Environmental Noise Impact Assessment*". Those Guidelines require an assessment to consider
the character of the noise and its maximum and underlying levels, not merely a change in average
level. The Assessment takes from them only the change-in-level criterion at §7.1, and does not
address:

- maximum levels (LAmax) at any receptor, at any time of day;
- the character of the noise the proposed use would generate;
- the underlying noise levels against which it would be heard; or
- the nature of the change in the noise environment.

Table 7.1 presents predicted levels averaged over a one-hour period. An average over an hour can
comprise periods of low noise and short periods of much higher noise and still yield the figure
reported. For a use of this kind — where the noise consists of voices, raised voices and
intermittent bursts of laughter and shouting — it is those short, loud events that are heard
inside a bedroom, and the Assessment does not quantify them.

This matters directly to the policy test. NPPF policy P3(2)(d) requires that development **not
result in levels of noise exposure which would have a significant observed adverse effect**. The
Assessment does not identify a significant observed adverse effect level for this source and
this context, and does not report the metric — maximum level — by which the effect at issue would
be judged. On the evidence submitted, the Council cannot conclude that the P3(2)(d) test is met.

**Request:** that the applicant be required to provide predicted **maximum** noise levels at each
receptor for the hours applied for, including after dark, together with the LOAEL and SOAEL
adopted for this source and their justification — **before determination**. *(This is a point of
insufficient evidence — category (B).)*

---

### 2. The baseline survey was taken at the roadside and not at the affected receptors, which overstates the existing noise environment

⟨Quote the survey location, show the mismatch, and state the direction of the error. Do not
assert a corrected figure.⟩

The survey position shown on Figure 4.1 is at the back of the footway on [road], approximately
[x] metres from the carriageway. The receptors the Assessment then reports levels *at* — R1 and
R2 — are set back behind [description], and R3 is a first-floor bedroom window facing the
proposed area.

Two consequences follow, and both run in the same direction:

- **(a)** a measurement position closer to the traffic records a higher ambient level than the
  receptors experience, so the **baseline is overstated**; and
- **(b)** the Assessment's own Table 4.2 shows ambient (LAeq) levels more than [x] dB above
  background (LA90) in both the daytime and night-time periods, which indicates an environment
  dominated by short, loud passing events with quieter periods between them. In those quieter
  periods — which is when the proposed use would be heard — the noise climate is materially
  lower than the reported average.

Because every predicted change in the Assessment is expressed relative to this baseline, an
overstated baseline **understates the change** the development would cause at every receptor.

**Request:** that baseline measurements be undertaken at, or properly corrected to, the receptor
façades and external amenity areas, with the quieter periods analysed separately, and the
assessment re-run on that basis — **before determination**. *(Category (B).)*

---

### 3. The conclusion depends on a margin smaller than the Assessment's own stated uncertainty

⟨A point made entirely from the applicant's document. Short, and hard to answer.⟩

The Assessment states at §5.6 that the prediction carries an uncertainty of **±[x] dB**. Its
conclusion at §8.2 rests on a predicted increase of **[y] dB** at R1, said to be below the
adopted criterion. Since [y] is smaller than [x], the Assessment's own figures are consistent
with an increase materially above the criterion it adopts.

**Request:** that the conclusion be re-stated across the range the stated uncertainty permits,
and the worst case within that range assessed — **before determination**. *(Category (B).)*

---

### 4. Mitigation is assumed to perform perfectly, and is not specified

The predicted levels at §7.3 credit the proposed [barrier/enclosure] with an attenuation of [x]
dB. The submitted drawings show [doors/gaps/a junction with the existing building] at [location],
and the Assessment provides no acoustic specification — no mass per unit area, no treatment of
joints or gaps, and no analysis of sound passing over or around the structure. Nor is performance
assessed at R3, a **first-floor** window, where a ground-level barrier of the height proposed
would provide substantially less benefit than at ground level.

**Request:** that the acoustic specification and the assumptions on gaps, doors and junctions be
provided, and performance assessed at upper-floor receptors — **before determination**; and, if
permission is granted, that the [barrier] be secured by condition to a stated specification with
verification before first use. *(Category (B), with (C) as the fallback.)*

---

### 5. The conclusion relies on management measures, which do not run with the land

The Assessment concludes at §8.4 that residual effects will be controlled through the Premises
Management Plan — supervision of the outdoor area, signage, and a staffed closing procedure.

A planning permission runs with the land and not with the operator. Measures that depend on a
particular operator's practices do not bind the use, and would not survive a change of operator.
Separately, supervision is a weak control over noise from groups of people, because a small
number of people behaving loudly is enough to cause disturbance and staff cannot prevent it as it
occurs.

The Framework's answer is the right one: **DM6(1)(c)** requires conditions to be "sufficiently
precise to make them capable of being complied with and enforced". Where the acceptability of the
scheme depends on a measure, that measure needs to be a condition, not a commitment.

**Request:** that any measure the Assessment's conclusion relies on be expressed as a precise,
enforceable condition — hours of use by area and by day, capacity fixed by number and by the
approved layout, a closure and clearance regime, and a boundary noise limit with a stated metric,
measurement position and period. Where an effect cannot be controlled by such a condition, that
element of the proposal should be refused. *(Category (C), escalating to (A) for any element
whose only proposed control is management.)*

---

### 6. Deliveries, servicing, waste collection and plant are not assessed

⟨Often the most conditionable point, and worth making even where the rest of the assessment
survives.⟩

The application would [extend hours / intensify the use], but the Assessment addresses only noise
from the outdoor area. It does not assess:

- delivery and servicing movements, or their timing;
- waste and bottle collection;
- the [refrigeration / extraction / air-handling] plant listed on the plant schedule, which is not
  assessed against **BS 4142** at all; or
- vehicle movements and dispersal from the site at closing time.

These are among the sources most likely to affect sleep, because they occur at the most sensitive
hours.

**Request:** that servicing, delivery, waste-collection, plant and dispersal noise be assessed —
**before determination** — and, if permission is granted, that hours and plant specifications be
secured by condition. *(Category (B), with (C) available for the plant and hours.)*

## Summary of requests

⟨Numbered, concrete, and grouped. Most end "before determination". This is the part the officer
lifts.⟩

1. That predicted **maximum** noise levels be provided at each receptor for the hours applied
   for, including after dark, with the **LOAEL and SOAEL** adopted for this source justified —
   before determination.
2. That the **baseline** be re-measured at, or corrected to, the receptor façades and external
   amenity areas, with the quieter periods analysed separately — before determination.
3. That the **character** of the noise be assessed, in accordance with the methodology the
   Assessment states it follows — before determination.
4. That the conclusion be re-stated across the **uncertainty** the Assessment itself declares —
   before determination.
5. That the **acoustic specification** of the proposed mitigation be provided and its performance
   assessed at **upper-floor** receptors — before determination.
6. That **deliveries, servicing, waste collection, plant and dispersal** be assessed — before
   determination.
7. That the application be **re-referred to Environmental Health** on the updated evidence, and
   not determined until their concerns are resolved.

## If the Council is nonetheless minded to approve

⟨Fallback safeguards. Precise enough to be enforced — this is where DM6(1)(c) is your friend
rather than theirs.⟩

1. **Hours** specified separately for the outdoor area, the indoor premises, and deliveries,
   servicing and waste collection, by day of the week.
2. **Capacity** of the outdoor area fixed by a stated maximum number of persons and by the
   approved layout drawing, with no alteration without further permission.
3. A **boundary noise limit** expressed with a metric, a measurement position, an averaging
   period and the operating condition to which it applies.
4. **Plant** limited to a rating level a stated number of decibels below the measured background
   at a stated position, with **verification testing after installation and before first use**.
5. The **mitigation structure** built to an approved acoustic specification, in place and verified
   before the outdoor area is first used.
6. A **closure and clearance regime** for the outdoor area, and a requirement that no drinks be
   taken outside after the stated time.
7. **Review**: the outdoor area permitted on a **temporary** basis in the first instance, so that
   the predicted levels can be tested against measured outcomes before any permanent permission.

Unless and until these matters are resolved, I object to the grant of permission.
