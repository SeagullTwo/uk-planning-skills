# Noise — the policy, standards and guidance catalogue

What to cite, and for what. Use this to attach a **specific** instrument to every confirmed
deficiency. Never cite "best practice" in the abstract, and never cite from memory — verify
against the source before a draft is sent.

> 2026 edition** (official PDF) on **1 September 2026**. Standards, guidance and policy
> instruments were re-verified against primary sources on **16 September 2026**. Editions turn
> over silently — **re-verify at the point of drafting**, and check the

## 1. The Framework — NPPF (17 August 2026 edition)

The August 2026 edition replaced sequential paragraph numbers with **coded policies in themed
chapters**. Noise sits in **Chapter 17, "Pollution, public protection and security"** (codes
`P1`–`P6`).

> **Superseded-citation trap.** Every pre-August-2026 paragraph number is stale. The
> noise-and-amenity paragraphs objectors and consultees have long cited — 191–194 in the
> December 2024 edition, 185/187 in 2021, 123 in 2012 — no longer exist as such. Expect to
> meet them in officer reports, consultee responses, appeal decisions and the applicant's own
> documents; **translate rather than repeat**, and where you quote a dated document that uses
> them, say which edition it was written under. The crosswalk lives in the
> **national-planning-policy** skill (`references/nppf-crosswalk-2026.md`). Applications
> **determined before 17 August 2026** were decided under the earlier edition — cite the
> edition in force at determination when discussing a past decision.

### P3 — Living conditions and pollution (the core noise policy)

**P3(1)** — the framing. Development proposals "should be appropriate for their location,
taking into account the likely effects (**including cumulative effects**) of pollution on
health, living conditions and the natural environment, whether these effects are **on or
off-site**; and whether this is as a result of the development itself or the product of
preexisting conditions in its vicinity." Sensitivity is expressly in scope: the assessment
must consider "the potential sensitivity of the site, and its intended occupiers and users, as
well as that of the environment and population in the surrounding area (including where
certain groups could be particularly vulnerable to its effects, such as children and older
people)."
- *Use for:* cumulative effects left out (**C7**); off-site effects treated as someone else's
  problem; sensitive receptors omitted (**A7**); vulnerable occupiers (schools, care homes,
  children's bedrooms) not considered.

**P3(2)(a)** — **Direction B, the occupiers of the new development.** Proposals should
"provide healthy living conditions for occupiers and users in terms of adequate access to
light and **avoiding exposure to levels of air, noise, artificial light or other sources of
pollution which could have an unacceptable adverse effect on health and the quality of life**.
Where necessary suitable mitigation measures should be incorporated to secure acceptable
living conditions."

**P3(2)(b)** — **Direction A, everyone else.** Proposals should "not give rise to, or
contribute to … **unacceptable levels of air, noise, artificial light, water, soil or other
forms of pollution on or beyond the site**. Adverse impacts of this nature should be **avoided
where possible, or else mitigated to an acceptable degree**." (The same limb also covers
daylight and sunlight for neighbours.)
- *Use for:* the primary test in any objection to a noise-generating development. Note the
  hierarchy — **avoid** first, mitigate only where avoidance is not possible.

**P3(2)(d)** — the noise-specific limb, and the strongest single hook in the catalogue.
Proposals should "**Mitigate and reduce to a minimum** potential adverse impacts resulting
from noise, **not result in levels of noise exposure which would have a significant observed
adverse effect**, and **maintain the character of tranquil areas** (those areas that have
remained relatively undisturbed by noise from human sources and are prized for their
recreational and amenity value for this reason)."
- *Use for:* **B10** (no LOAEL/SOAEL identified — the policy is expressed in the language of
  observed effect levels, so an assessment that never identifies them cannot demonstrate
  compliance); "reduce to a minimum" against mitigation that stops at "acceptable"; tranquil
  areas where the local plan or evidence base identifies them.

**P3(3)** — the other-regimes assumption. "In applying this policy, it should be assumed,
**unless there is clear evidence to the contrary**, that separate regulatory regimes for the
control of pollution will operate effectively, as set out in policy DM7."
- *Use with care:* this cuts against objectors. See **D6** — it is rebuttable, but only on
  evidence.

**Glossary (Annex B)** — "**Significant observed adverse effect level**: This is the level of
noise exposure above which significant adverse effects on health and quality of life occur."

### P4 — Impact of development on existing activities (agent of change) — **Direction B only**

**P4(1)** — "Existing businesses, community facilities, public services and defence and
security activities **should not have unreasonable restrictions placed on their current or
permitted operation as a result of development being approved after they were established**."
Development proposals "should be capable of being integrated effectively with existing
business, community and public service activities and infrastructure in their vicinity
(including, but not limited to, uses such as **pubs, music venues**, places of worship, sports
clubs, blue light services, defence, electricity network infrastructure and industrial and
waste sites)."

**P4(2)** — where an existing activity "could have a significant adverse effect on a proposed
new development in its vicinity", proposals should **(a)** identify the nature of the potential
impacts, "informed where necessary by early discussions between the applicant (i.e. the 'agent
of change') and those existing uses which could be affected"; and **(b)** "be able to
demonstrate that **suitable mitigation can be provided before first occupation** of the
development, if it is to be acceptable. **Planning conditions or obligations should be used to
secure agreed mitigation measures.**"

**P4(3)** — scope. The policy "applies to new development involving **changes of use** of land
and property, as well as to new construction", and "**Both lawful current and permitted levels
of operation** of existing activities should be taken into account when applying this policy."
- *Use for:* the whole of section **F**. P4(3) is the sharp edge — the assessment must address
  what the existing activity is *entitled* to do, not what it currently does.

Say this to yourself before pitching an **(A)**. **One route does exist:** **DP3(3)** *is* a refusal directive, so where the noise failing is also a design failing — most obviously **D4**, an acoustic case that works only with the windows shut, against DP3(2)(b) on overheating, or a liveability failure under DP3(2)(a) — the point can be pitched through DP3(3) as well as P3. Use it only where the design limb genuinely bites; stretching it will cost more than it gains. |
| **HC6 / TC-chapter policies** | Where the proposal *is* the community facility or town-centre use, expect the applicant to run its own benefits case; the balance is struck against those benefits, not in a vacuum. |

September 2026) — cite by paragraph ID **and** date, e.g. *Paragraph: 004 Reference ID:
30-004-20190722*.

> **⏳ Currency caution — the cross-references are now broken, not merely stale.** Re-checked
> 16 September 2026: the page is **unchanged since 22 July 2019**, and its "related policy"
> references still point at **2019** NPPF paragraph numbers — against a Framework that, since
> 17 August 2026, **has no paragraph numbers at all**. A revised PPG has been promised and is
> undated.
>

> direction. The PPG's **substance** — the effect-level hierarchy, the factors that make noise

> **signposting**. So: cite the PPG by paragraph ID and date for method and substance, cite the
> NPPF by its codes, and never repeat a PPG cross-reference as though it located anything in
> the current Framework. Expect applicants and officers to carry the stale numbers across
> unnoticed.

## 3. National noise policy

**Noise Policy Statement for England (NPSE), Defra, March 2010** (PB13750) — ⏳ current at
verification; not withdrawn or superseded. Sets the long-term vision "to promote good health
and a good quality of life through the effective management of noise", and the three aims,
through the effect-level framework the PPG carries into planning:

- **avoid** significant adverse impacts on health and quality of life;
- **mitigate and minimise** adverse impacts on health and quality of life; and
- where possible, **contribute to the improvement** of health and quality of life.

*Use for:* the "reduce to a minimum" and "avoid where possible" arguments in **P3(2)(b)** and
**(d)**, and as the source of the NOEL/LOAEL/SOAEL concepts. Note that NPSE deliberately does
**not** set numeric SOAEL values — they are for the assessor to justify in context, which is
exactly why **B10** bites when the assessment simply borrows a number.

> **⏳ A citation trap on NPSE, and how to handle it.** The phrase "Noise Policy Statement for
> England" **does not appear anywhere in the August 2026 Framework** (checked across the whole
> text). NPSE has not been withdrawn and decision-makers do still apply it by name — but its
> connection to the Framework's SOAEL language now runs only through the **PPG**, which is
> itself stale. So build **B10** on the instrument that is unambiguously current and binding:
> **P3(2)(d)**, which puts "significant observed adverse effect" into policy, with **Annex B**
> defining the level. Then use **PPG 30-004** for the effect-level hierarchy and NPSE for the
> concepts' provenance and the avoid/mitigate/improve aims. Cited in that order the point does
> not depend on any single instrument surviving the next edition.

## 4. Standards and technical guidance

Cite the standard **that fits the source**, and check whether it has been applied in full.

Requires the **specific sound level** to be corrected for **acoustic features** (tonality, impulsivity, intermittency, other characteristics) to give a **rating level**, compared with the **background** (LA90); requires **context** to be considered and **uncertainty** to be addressed. ### ⏳ The BS 8233 revision — what is actually happening

A revision has been drafted and **has not been published**. The sequence, verified 16 September
2026: a draft for public comment issued in **June 2025**, comments closed **14 September 2025**,
and the profession objected substantially — including on the proposed two-step process, the
move to L<sub>den</sub>/L<sub>night</sub> metrics, the removal of room- and activity-specific
criteria, and the removal of night-time event (L<sub>Amax</sub>) guidance. **No revised edition
has been published and no publication date is fixed.**

Three consequences:
- **Cite BS 8233:2014.** Pages and articles referring to "BS 8233:2026" are anticipating a
  document that does not exist. Citing it would be wrong and checkable.
- **Expect to meet the draft's content in applicants' documents.** A consultant may reason from
  the draft. That is not illegitimate, but the draft is not the standard, and a conclusion that
  depends on the draft's departures — particularly the removal of night-event criteria — should
  be identified as resting on an unpublished document.
- **Re-check before anything deadline-critical.** This is the item in this file most likely to
  move, and it would change the Direction B internal targets.

**Choosing the standard for people noise.** There is **no British or international standard, and
no national guidance, specifically for assessing patron, crowd, outdoor-drinking or dispersal
noise** — re-verified across the IOA, ANC, CIEH, the Institute of Licensing and Defra on 16
September 2026, and nothing has been published on it between 2023 and 2026. **BS 4142 expressly
excludes music and entertainment noise; BS 8233 excludes sources with a specific character**;
and the PPG mentions customer noise without supplying either a method or a criterion. The
working references remain the IOA's 2003 pubs-and-clubs guide and the 1995 Pop Code.

That absence is a fact worth stating in a representation, because it cuts in a particular
direction: where no prescribed methodology exists, the burden falls on the applicant to justify
the method actually used and its criteria (**E3**), and an assessment that simply borrows the
numeric criteria of a steady-source standard has not discharged it (**B3**, **B4**). In practice
authorities fill the vacuum with their own criteria, often a subjective "inaudible at the
boundary" condition — which is worth testing against **DM6(1)(c)** before you ask for one
(**G3**).

**What has changed on this topic is the policy hook, not the method.** The methodology has been
static since 2003; **P4** (17 August 2026) and the National Licensing Policy Framework (26
November 2025) are new. Argue the policy, not a new standard that does not exist.

## 5. Law and the other regimes — the boundary

- **Town and Country Planning Act 1990, s.70(2)** and **Planning and Compulsory Purchase Act
  2004, s.38(6)** — determination in accordance with the development plan unless material
  considerations indicate otherwise. Take the full statement of the decision-making core from
  the **national-planning-policy** skill rather than restating it here.
- **Environmental Protection Act 1990, Part III (ss.79–82)** — statutory nuisance, including
  noise emitted from premises so as to be prejudicial to health or a nuisance. **Signpost
  only.** The statutory-nuisance test is not the planning test, and the planning test is the
  lower bar: a level well below what would be held a statutory nuisance can still be an
  unacceptable effect on living conditions under **P3**. Say that if the applicant equates
  them — but do not run the objection on nuisance law.
- ***Roper v Tussauds Theme Parks Ltd* [2007] EWHC 624 (Admin)** — a **statutory nuisance**
  case, cited in this field for the proposition that it is not appropriate to look only at the
  absolute level of noise: the **character** of the noise must be considered. ⏳ **Cite with
  care and only where it earns its place:** it is persuasive on the character point (**B7**),
  it is not a planning authority, and it should be introduced as such. Verify the citation
  before quoting it.
- **Licensing Act 2003** — a **separate regime**, with its own objectives (including the
  prevention of public nuisance), its own parties, a short representation window and its own
  procedure. Noise disturbance can be relevant to both regimes, but a planning representation
  is not a licensing representation. Where a parallel licensing application appears to exist,
  tell the user and point them at the licensing authority's process — and remember **DM7(2)**:
  planning should not duplicate or extend licensing controls, though it still decides whether
  the **use of land** is acceptable.
  - ⏳ The **s.182 statutory guidance** under the Act is volatile — it was revised twice in
    2026 and again on **15 September 2026**. Never cite a remembered version; check the date.
- **National Licensing Policy Framework (DBT / Home Office, 26 November 2025)** — ⏳ current,
  and **more useful to a planning representation than its title suggests**, for three reasons:
  - **It addresses beer gardens and licensed pavement areas directly**, which is the source
    with no standard behind it and therefore the one where any authoritative statement is worth
    having.
  - **It applies the agent-of-change principle within licensing**, and says it should work in
    harmony with planning policy — so the principle now runs through both regimes rather than
    being a planning-only concept the licensing authority can disclaim.
  - **It states that licensing decisions do not undermine planning decisions, which take
    primacy.** This is the cleanest available answer to the applicant who says noise is a
    licensing matter, and it is the government's own statement rather than an objector's
    argument. Pair it with **D6 limb (a)**: the regimes ask different questions, and planning's
    is the one that decides whether the use of the land is acceptable.

  Verify the current version and the section numbering before quoting it — this instrument is
  recent and the licensing guidance around it has moved repeatedly.

## 6. The local tier

National policy is the floor, not the case. Always add:

- the **adopted development plan's** policies on noise, pollution and residential amenity — the
  Council's own policies, which have primacy (use the **policy-compliance-assessment** skill to
  identify and verify them, and check **Annex A(2)** on the weight given to plan policies
  materially inconsistent with the Framework's decision-making policies);
- any **neighbourhood plan** policies on amenity, hours or the evening economy;
- the authority's **local technical guidance** on noise and planning, and its **validation
  list** requirements for noise assessments;
- on an amendment, the **noise conditions of the parent permission** — what protection was
  secured, and what this application would do to it.
