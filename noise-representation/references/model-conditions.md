# Noise conditions — what holds, and what does not

A **(C)** ask is only as good as the condition behind it. This file is the drafting reference
for the conditions a representation should request, and — equally important — the ones it
should not, because asking for an unenforceable condition converts a good point into a weak one.

Every condition must meet the four tests in **DM6(1)**: necessary; relevant to the development
and to planning generally; **sufficiently precise to be capable of being complied with and
enforced**; and reasonable in all other respects. **DM6(4)**: an obligation only where a
condition cannot do the job.

> **Use these as shapes, not as text to paste.** Each needs the specific figures, drawing
> numbers, areas and times for the application in front of you. A condition requested with the
> blanks left in tells the case officer the representation was not written about this site.

---

## 1. The shapes that work

The pattern across conditions that have survived challenge is consistent: they control
**something observable** — a physical thing that is either there or not, or an activity
happening at a time when it either is or is not permitted. They do not require a judgement to
be made later, and they do not depend on measuring something at the moment it happens.

### A. A physical measure, tied to a drawing, with a trigger and a retention requirement

The strongest form. It specifies what, where, how big, by reference to what, by when, and for
how long.

> In accordance with the recommendation in the approved [*report title, author, date,
> reference*], a [*height*] metre high acoustic [*fence / barrier*] shall be erected along the
> [*identify boundary*] as shown on approved drawing [*number and revision*]. The
> [*use / development*] shall not [*commence / be occupied / be brought into use*] until the
> [*fence / barrier*] is in place, and it shall be retained thereafter.

Why each limb is there:
- **Tied to the approved report and the approved drawing** — so the height, line and
  specification cannot drift.
- **A trigger before first use** — so it is in place before the impact starts, not afterwards.
- **"Retained thereafter"** — without it the condition is discharged the day it is built.

Add the acoustic specification (mass per unit area, no gaps, treatment at junctions and gates)
where the assessment relied on a particular performance — see **D1**. And check **D8**: if the
thing sits on someone else's land, or the people it protects would have to maintain it, say so.

### B. Hours by area or facility, by day, with a clearance time and an express access
prohibition

The single most useful family of noise conditions, and the one most often drafted too loosely.
Four limbs, all of them needed:

> The [*identify the specific area — e.g. the external terrace, the beer garden, the
> multi-use games area*] shall only be open to [*customers / members of the public / users*]
> between [*time*] and [*time*] on [*days*], and between [*time*] and [*time*] on [*days*].
> It shall not be accessed by [*customers / users*] outside those times, and shall be cleared
> of all [*customers / users*] by [*time*].

- **By area, not by premises** — an hours condition on "the site" does not stop the outdoor
  area being used when the indoor area is open.
- **By day** — weekends and Sundays are where the respite argument (**C9**) lives.
- **An express prohibition on out-of-hours access** — closing time and the time the last person
  leaves are different, and it is the second one the neighbours hear.
- **A clearance time** — the enforceable version of the above.

Where the objection is **loss of respite**, ask additionally for what the respite condition
should protect: a closure day ("the site shall not be used for any purpose at any time on
Sundays and Bank Holidays"), or seasonally differentiated hours.

### C. Staff and servicing hours, separately from customer hours

Customers and staff are different populations arriving at different times, and deliveries are
different again. One set of hours for all three is either too tight to be reasonable or too
loose to protect anyone.

> No [*deliveries to / collections from*] the premises, and no [*bottle / refuse*] emptying or
> collection, shall take place outside [*time*] to [*time*] on [*days*], or at any time on
> [*days*].

Expect the applicant to trade morning servicing access against evening closure — that is a
legitimate exchange, and a representation that anticipates it reads as constructive.

### D. Activity prohibitions, for things that cannot be controlled by level

Where a source cannot be sensibly limited by a number, prohibit it:

> No live or recorded music shall be played in [*the identified area*] at any time.

> No [*amplification equipment / speakers / public-address system*] shall be used in
> [*the identified area*] at any time.

These reach activity the licensing regime does not (see **D6**), which is part of why they are
worth asking for.

### E. Lighting hours, separated from hours of use

Floodlighting and external lighting keep an area in use and draw people to it. Separate the two:

> The [*floodlighting / external lighting*] to [*the identified facility*] shall not operate
> after [*time*] on [*days*] or after [*time*] on [*days*].

### F. Plant: specification, then verification, then maintenance

> The [*plant / extraction system / air source heat pump*] hereby approved shall be installed
> in accordance with the acoustic specification in [*approved report*], and shall not be brought
> into use until a post-installation verification assessment, undertaken by a competent person
> at [*identified measurement position*], has been submitted to and approved in writing by the
> Local Planning Authority demonstrating that the [*rating level / level*] does not exceed
> [*the stated criterion*]. The plant shall thereafter be maintained in accordance with the
> approved specification.

The maintenance limb is the answer to **C8** — plant assessed as new and only as new.

### G. Hours for unstaffed equipment accessible to the public

Worth knowing because the standard objection to it — "the equipment cannot be switched off" —
does not work. A condition limiting the **hours of use** is enforceable even where the machine
runs continuously, because whether it is being used at a given time is observable on a visit,
and there are ways to prevent use short of disabling it.

### H. On a multi-unit or outline scheme: hours approved per unit before first use

> Prior to the commencement of the use of any individual [*use class*] unit, details of the
> proposed opening and trading hours for that unit shall be submitted to and approved in
> writing by the Local Planning Authority. The unit shall thereafter operate only within the
> approved hours.

The legitimate use of a deferred condition: the end operators are genuinely unknown, and the
condition reserves the judgement rather than giving it away. Contrast §2 below.

## 2. What not to ask for

### Do not ask for the assessment to be done later

The most common way a noise deficiency is made to disappear, and the one that most often fails
— see **G5**. A condition requiring a noise assessment, survey or mitigation scheme to be
submitted after permission cannot be shown to be capable of being discharged, because the
assessment might reveal a level that cannot be mitigated, or mitigation the building cannot
accommodate, or works that are not permitted development. By then permission has been granted.

**The distinction that keeps this honest.** Deferral is proper where the performance standard is
understood and only the workmanship is open — internal sound insulation between floors to a
stated standard is the standing example, and conditions of that kind are routinely accepted.
It is improper where the deferred document would determine **whether the development is
acceptable at all**. Say which of the two you say this is.

### Be careful asking for a bare numerical limit

A boundary or rating-level limit looks like the ideal condition and sometimes is — but it has
three failure modes:

- **It may be refused as unreasonable** if the evidence does not show it can actually be met.
  A limit nobody has demonstrated is achievable is a condition the applicant cannot comply with.
- **It may be offered as a substitute for hours control rather than an addition to it.** If the
  hours are the point, say that the limit is not an alternative to them.
- **It reaches plant and equipment, not people.** This is the most frequent error. A condition
  limiting noise from "plant and machinery" does nothing about patrons, dispersal, deliveries or
  car doors — and where the objection is people noise, accepting a plant-noise condition as the
  answer concedes the case. Check what any offered limit actually bites on.

Where a limit is right, express it with **all five**: the metric, the limit, the measurement
location, the time period, and the reference condition (what it is measured against, and in what
circumstances).

> Noise emitted from [*operations within the site / the plant*], measured as a rating level in
> accordance with [*the applicable standard, cited by its correct full title and edition*] at
> [*identified measurement position and height*], shall not exceed [*the background sound level
> that would otherwise prevail in the absence of noise from the site*] by more than [*figure*]
> dB during [*stated period*].

⏳ **Get the standard's title and edition right.** Conditions circulate with mis-stated titles,
and a condition that mis-cites the standard it depends on invites argument at the discharge
stage. Check the current title and edition in
[`national-guidance.md`](national-guidance.md) before using one.

### Do not ask for "inaudible"

A condition that noise "shall be inaudible at the boundary", or "shall not cause disturbance to
neighbouring occupiers", is the authority's habitual answer to a source with no standard behind
it. It fails **DM6(1)(c)**: audibility is not a measurable quantity, it varies with the listener
and the background, and there is no method for determining a breach. Ask for hours, capacity,
clearance and a specification instead.

### Avoid tailpieces

"…or such other details as may be agreed in writing with the Local Planning Authority" attached
to the end of a condition should be resisted. It risks significant changes being made outside
the statutory route for varying conditions, depriving third parties of the opportunity to
comment — and where the condition is the thing making a noisy scheme acceptable, that is the
whole protection.

### Do not ask for a trial period without its machinery

See **G6**. If a trial is genuinely appropriate, the condition must fix the duration, the
monitoring method, the metric, the locations, the **pre-existing baseline** the trial will be
judged against, and what happens if the result is adverse. A baseline that was not established
before the trial started cannot be reconstructed afterwards, and without one the trial cannot
answer anything.

## 3. Before you ask for any of them

- **Is it necessary?** If the scheme is acceptable without it, asking for it weakens the points
  that matter (**G4**).
- **Does it control the land, or the operator?** A condition that depends on how a particular
  proprietor behaves is not a control on the use — see **D3**.
- **Does it reach the source you are actually worried about?** Plant conditions do not reach
  people; indoor conditions do not reach the forecourt; a cap on one outdoor area does not
  reach patrons who can walk into another.
- **Could it be complied with?** A condition the applicant cannot meet is not a condition.
- **Would you rather have this than a refusal?** Say so honestly. A representation that asks
  for refusal *and* sets out the conditions it wants if the authority is minded to approve is
  more useful to a case officer than one that asks only for refusal — put them under a clear
  "if minded to approve" heading so the primary ask is not diluted.
