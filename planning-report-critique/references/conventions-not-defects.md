# Conventions that are not defects

Reference file for `planning-report-critique`. Read before recording any reasoning defect.

Officer reports, committee reports and appeal statements are written to professional
convention, not to formal-logic standards. Several conventions read as reasoning failures
when tested strictly, and are not. Each entry below has been seen asserted as a defect in
testing, and each is orthodox practice.

## Conditional reasoning is not circular reasoning

> "No obligation has been completed, as the scheme is recommended for refusal."

Conventional and transparent. The report states why the obligation is absent, and usually
what would cure it.

It becomes circular **only** if the absence is then relied on as an independent reason for
refusal without that explanation. Check whether the explanation is there before recording
anything.

## Differential treatment of topics is normal

Conditioning one matter while refusing on another is not inconsistency, provided a reason is
given for each. Drainage conditioned "given the low flood risk" while ecology surveys are
required before determination is a reasoned distinction.

The defect is unexplained differential treatment, not differential treatment.

## Two propositions that differ are not a contradiction

> "Sufficient space is available for the parking required"
> "The applicant would need to demonstrate that turning within the site is achievable"

Different findings. Meeting a numeric standard while failing to demonstrate manoeuvring is
coherent. Before recording a contradiction, state both propositions in full and check they
are actually about the same thing.

## An authority may speak to its own knowledge

A statement of the authority's own housing land supply position, its committee procedure, its
consultation history, or the adopted status of its own plan is not an unevidenced assertion.
It is not required to file evidence of matters within its own competence.

**Absence of a supporting document on the file is not contradiction.** Challenge such a
statement only where something on the file contradicts it.

## Pre-application advice is a ceiling, not an assurance

> "1.5 storeys would likely be the most the site could accommodate"

Sets an upper bound. It does not warrant that any 1.5-storey scheme is acceptable, and a
report is not obliged to treat a scheme that followed the advice as thereby approved. A
report that credits the change and then finds harm elsewhere has engaged with it.

## Standard-form reasons carry their context

Template refusal wording can read loosely in isolation while being well understood locally
and legally sufficient. Criticise the substance rather than the drafting, unless the drafting
itself makes the reason unenforceable or unclear on its face.

## Weight the author reached differently

A report that gives a benefit moderate weight where you would give it substantial weight has
not erred. Weight is for the decision-maker.

The finding is available only where the weight given is **unexplained**, or where a statutory
or policy weighting was required and not applied — heritage "considerable importance and
weight", development plan primacy, an engaged presumption. Disagreement about the amount is
a position to argue.

## Non-determination of a matter left to another regime

Building regulations, licensing, environmental permitting and highways adoption have their
own consent routes. A report that identifies a matter as controlled elsewhere and says so has
addressed it.

The defect is failing to explain why, or treating a planning matter as though it were
someone else's regime.

---

**The test in every case:** is the reasoning **wrong**, or is it merely **not how you would
have put it**?

Only the first belongs in a list of findings. The second, if worth saying at all, is an
observation — and it needs the one-line reason explaining why it is not a finding.
