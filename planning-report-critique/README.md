# planning-report-critique

**What is wrong with this report, and does it matter enough to act on?**

For a reader deciding whether to challenge a decision, appeal it, respond at committee, or
send a draft back.

## A presentation layer, not a second analysis

This skill runs `planning-report-quality` and reports a filtered, ordered subset of what it
finds. It adds **judgement about what to raise**, and the discipline to leave the rest out.

That division is deliberate. A defect list built independently of the measurement would drift
from it, and you would end up maintaining two definitions of "defect".

## Findings and observations

A true observation is never a fault in the author. Identifying something correctly is not a
defect — whether it is relevant, and what weight it carries, is a separate judgement made
later and by someone else.

So the output separates:

- **Findings** — the reasoning is wrong on the law, contradicted by the evidence, or omits
  something material.
- **Observations** — true, possibly worth a sentence, but the decision does not turn on it.

**Every observation states in one line why it is not a finding.** Without that rule, an
assessment relabels wholesale and looks disciplined without having thought.

## The raise test

Something becomes a finding only where all three hold:

- it is wrong, immaterial-but-weighted, omitted, or mis-weighted — not merely different from
  how you would have put it;
- it could change the outcome; and
- the author gave no adequate reason for the approach taken.

Where a reason is given and it is rational, the point has been **addressed, not ignored**.

## Severity is derived, not assigned

Severity falls out of the quality pass — accuracy verdict × load-bearing verdict — rather
than being judged by feel. An incorrect claim the recommendation depends on is an A whatever
its subject; a mis-weighted background claim is a C.

## Why the conventions reference exists

`references/conventions-not-defects.md` lists things that read as reasoning failures and are
not: conditional reasoning mistaken for circular, differential treatment of topics, two
propositions that merely differ, an authority speaking to its own knowledge, pre-application
advice read as an assurance.

Every entry was written after being asserted as a defect in testing. Reports are written to
professional convention, not to formal-logic standards, and a critique that does not know the
difference produces a long list that will not survive contact with the author.

## "It is sound" is a valid output

Where the report holds up, the skill says so. A critique that manufactures findings to look
thorough is worse than useless: it spends the reader's credibility on points that will not
stand.

Every finding also states **what would cure it** — which is the test of whether the finding
is real. A defect nobody can articulate a cure for is usually a disagreement about judgement
wearing a defect's clothes.
