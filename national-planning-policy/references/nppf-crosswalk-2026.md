# NPPF crosswalk: December 2024 paragraphs → August 2026 policy codes

**Purpose.** Two uses only: (1) **retrospective work** — applications determined before
17 August 2026 were decided under the December 2024 (or earlier) edition, and must be read
against the edition in force at determination; this table translates between the two;
(2) **re-verification aid** — while the repo's catalogues carry both numbering systems in
their history, this table shows where each concept the repo cites now lives. It covers the
December 2024 paragraph numbers **this repo actually cited** (the shared decision-making
core plus the four topic catalogues), not the whole Framework.

**Source.** The official PDF of the National Planning Policy Framework published
**17 August 2026** (assets.publishing.service.gov.uk, 130 pp; download magic-byte
verified), read against the repo's stored December 2024 citations. Verified **18 August
2026**. At that date the gov.uk landing page offered the PDF only — the HTML "accessible
version" was pending (⏳ re-check).

**⏳ Discipline.** This table is a snapshot, not a licence to cite from it. Before any
citation leaves a skill, verify the policy code *and its current wording* against the live
text per the verification protocol in `../SKILL.md`. Where the notes below say a concept
**changed in substance**, do not present the new code as a mere renumbering of the old
paragraph — describe the new mechanism.

## The decision-making core

| Dec 2024 para | Concept | Aug 2026 policy | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | s.38(6)/s.70(2) restated; Framework a material consideration | Introduction paras 1, 3 (footnotes 1–2) | Wording strengthened: the Framework is now "a material consideration of critical importance" (Intro 1). The Introduction "does not contain substantive policy" (Intro 4) — cite it for the statutory frame only. |
| 10–11 | Presumption in favour of sustainable development | S3–S6 | **Changed in substance.** The presumption is now location-based (within/outside settlements), not plan-status-based. See rows below. |
| 11(c) | Approve plan-compliant development without delay | S3(1)(c) | Now requires accordance with **both** an up-to-date plan **and** the Framework's decision-making policies: "should be approved without delay". |
| 11(d) | The "tilted balance" where policies absent/out-of-date | **No direct successor** — nearest: S4(1) (within settlements), S5 (outside) | **Changed in substance.** The out-of-date-policies trigger and the "significantly and demonstrably outweigh" test are gone. S4(1): within settlements, approve "unless the benefits … would be substantially outweighed by any adverse effects". S5: outside settlements, only listed categories should be approved. A stronger presumption within settlements (unconditional on plan status); a closed list outside them. |
| 12 | Presumption "does not change the statutory status of the development plan" | **No direct successor** — nearest: Introduction para 3 + footnote 2 | s.38(6) is statute and unchanged; Intro fn 2 confirms the Framework "does not displace the statutory obligations". **But note Annex A(2)**: plan policies "materially inconsistent" with the new national decision-making policies get "very limited weight" unless examined against this Framework — a substantive shift in how plan/Framework conflict is weighed. |
| footnote 7 | Protected-asset list disapplying the tilted balance | Footnote 24 to S1 (plan-making); for decisions, S4(2)(c)/S5(2) + the specific refusal policies | **Changed in substance.** The list survives (habitats sites, SSSIs, Green Belt, Local Green Space, National Landscapes, National Parks/Broads, Heritage Coast, irreplaceable habitats, designated heritage assets, flood/coastal-change risk areas) but only as a *plan-making* footnote. In decisions the presumption yields where a proposal would "fail to comply with one of the national decision-making policies which state that development proposals should be refused in specific circumstances" (e.g. N6, GB6, HC8, F6–F7, HE6(5), TR6(4)). |
| footnote 8 | 5YHLS / HDT-below-75% out-of-date trigger | S5(1)(j) with footnotes 26–27; Annex D paras 8–9, 12(c); GB7 footnote 41; Annex A(3) | **Changed in substance.** No longer engages a general tilted balance. Lack of a five-year supply or HDT < 75% (Annex D 12(c) *deems* unmet need) instead qualifies housing as a category that "should be approved" **outside settlements** under S5(1)(j) (if well-related to a settlement) and on grey belt (GB7, fn 41). Annex A(3) shields recently adopted plans from the standard method counting as unmet need. |
| 48 | Decision-taking restatement of s.38(6) | Introduction para 3 | Same statutory frame; see para 2 row. |
| 49 | Weight to emerging plans | DM4(1) | Limbs (a) stage and (b) unresolved objections unchanged; limb (c) now "degree of compliance … with the policies for plan-making in this Framework" (was consistency with the Framework generally). Prematurity refusals: DM4(2)–(3). |
| 57 | Conditions — the six tests | DM6(1), with DM6(2)–(3) | **Changed in expression** (substance intact): four limbs — "necessary to make the development acceptable in planning terms; relevant to the development and to planning considerations generally; sufficiently precise to make them capable of being complied with and enforced; and reasonable in all other respects". New: no money-payment or land-transfer conditions (DM6(2)); national model conditions preferred, pre-commencement conditions avoided unless clearly justified (DM6(3)). |
| 58 | Obligations — CIL reg 122(2) three tests | DM6(4) | **Changed in substance of the text** (not of the law): the NPPF no longer restates the three tests; DM6(4) says obligations "should only be used where it is not possible to address potential unacceptable impacts through a planning condition", with national model obligations preferred. The reg 122(2) tests remain law — cite the regulation directly. |

## Flood risk (cited by `flood-representation`)

| Dec 2024 para | Concept | Aug 2026 policy | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 170 | Steer development from risk; safe for lifetime without increasing risk elsewhere | Chapter 18 objective; F7(1)–(2) | F7(1): proposals "should not present a risk from flooding to potential occupiers, users, or visitors, and should not increase flood risk elsewhere". |
| 173 | Sequential, risk-based approach applies to individual applications | F5 (with F4(3)) | Test applies "in areas known to be at risk now or in the future from any form of flooding" (F5(2)) — surface water still in scope. |
| 174 | The Sequential Test | F5(1) | Same aim ("steer new development to areas with the lowest risk of flooding from any source"). **New limit:** the search area "should not be greater than the anticipated catchment of the development" (F5(1)). |
| 175 | Carve-out where FRA shows no built development at risk | F5(2)(b) | Expanded: adds a surface-water-only route via layout/design/mitigation (F5(2)(b)(ii)); F5(2)(c) newly exempts householder development, small non-residential extensions (<250m²) and most changes of use. |
| 177–179 | The Exception Test (both parts) | F6(1)(b)–(2) | **Changed in substance.** Now three criteria, all required: wider sustainability benefits outweighing flood risk; safe for lifetime per F7; not increasing risk elsewhere and where possible reducing it. Applicability is set by **Annex F table 3** (vulnerability/zone matrix now inside the Framework, formerly PPG territory), and F6(1)(a) adds outright refusal where the use is incompatible with the zone. |
| 181 | Site-specific requirements (within-site sequential, resistance/resilience, residual risk, safe access) | F7(2)(a)–(e) | Same five strands; now framed as "it should be refused unless" all are met. SuDS moved to F8. |
| footnote 63 | When a site-specific FRA is required | F4(1)–(2) | Promoted from footnote to policy. **Note:** Flood Zone 3 is now split into **3a and 3b** (Annex F table 1); FRA required for all development in Zones 2, 3a, 3b, and in Zone 1 for sites ≥1 ha, critical-drainage land, or land identified as at risk from any source now or in future. |
| 182 | SuDS | F8 | Strengthened: SuDS "should" be incorporated (proportionately) and **designed in accordance with the National Standards for Sustainable Drainage Systems** (F8(2)(a) — new), with maintenance arrangements and, for major development, LLFA advice. Watercourse-enclosure/deculverting policy added (F8(3)). |

## Where the presumption interacts with these chapters

The old "footnote 7 disapplication" question ("does the tilted balance apply?") is now
asked differently: identify whether the site is within or outside a settlement (S4 vs S5),
then whether any **refusal-directive policy** in the tables above (N6, F6–F7, TR6(4),
HE6(5), GB6, HC8) is engaged — if so, the S4/S5 balance yields (S4(2)(c), S5(2)). See the
decision-making core in `../SKILL.md`.
